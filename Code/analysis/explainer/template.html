  • Exploratory. All four cells have been examined, so none is an untouched test set. Nothing here shows how a new cell would behave.

Part 1

From files to a clean table

Step 1 · Data processing

What did we start with?

Four recordings, {{n_ops}} timed operations, and one rule for turning them into cycles.

The data are NASA’s battery aging recordings for four lithium-ion cells. Each cell’s file is a long list of operations in time order. An operation is a charge ({{n_ops_charge}} in total), a discharge ({{n_ops_discharge}}) or an impedance sweep ({{n_ops_impedance}}). Charges and discharges carry sampled voltage, current and temperature, and every operation carries a timestamp.

We never edit these files. The analysis uses a fixed copy, commit {{source_commit}} of the project’s source repository, and checks every raw file’s SHA-256 checksum before anything runs.

B0005, B0006 and B0007 ran on one schedule: {{dis_main}} discharges and {{imp_main}} impedance sweeps each. B0018 ran later with {{dis_b0018}} discharges and only {{imp_b0018}} sweeps, which matters in the next step, because the sweeps are what supply two of the nine numbers.

The source loader then skips each cell’s first discharge (its own note says that charge was a partial start-up charge). That leaves {{n_cycles}} of the {{n_raw_discharges}} recorded discharges, so cycle numbers start at 2.

Step 2 · Data processing

Which charge, discharge and impedance sweep belong together?

Each discharge becomes one cycle. It is paired with the charge just before it and with the impedance sweep nearest in time, which is sometimes a later one.

A cycle in this analysis is one discharge plus the information around it:

  • 1The label. The discharge’s measured capacity in ampere-hours. State of health is capacity ÷ 2 Ah × 100.
  • 2The charge. The closest charge operation before the discharge.
  • 3The impedance sweep. The sweep closest in time to the discharge, before or after it. The loader does not check which side it falls on.
  • 4The interval. Hours from the end of the previous discharge to the start of this one. It includes the charge in between, so it is not pure rest.

One edge case: cycles 89 and 90 of B0005, B0006 and B0007 share a single charge, because no charge happened between those two discharges.

Why the side of the sweep matters

If you wanted to use a number at some moment, anything recorded after that moment would not exist yet. The next chart counts how far away the nearest impedance sweep is, for every cycle.

For {{eis_after_charge}} of {{n_cycles}} cycles ({{eis_after_charge_pct}}) the nearest sweep started after the charge had already ended, and for {{eis_after_discharge}} ({{eis_after_discharge_pct}}) it started after the discharge had begun. That is allowed for describing what happened, but it means Re and Rct could not be known at the end of charge. Chapter 5 returns to this.

Step 3 · Data processing

How do waveforms become nine numbers?

Each number comes from a plain rule applied to a recorded curve. Nothing is learned from the data to produce them.

A charge or discharge is stored as hundreds to thousands of samples of voltage, current and temperature. The source repository’s extraction code turns them into nine numbers per cycle. Below, pick a cycle and step through the rules. Every value was re-derived here from the raw recording with that same code, and each matches the saved table.

All nine numbers at a glance

This table lists what each feature measures, its range in the data, and whether its value could be known at two moments in a cycle. The last two columns are explained in chapter 5.

Does a setting of the rule change the answer?

The charge-curve peak is found on a 10 mV voltage grid. Was that choice important? The saved audit repeats the peak with 5 mV and 20 mV grids and compares how the cycles rank.

Step 4 · Data processing

Do any cycles look wrong?

Five charge records lack the normal voltage climb. The analysis leaves their four charging numbers blank rather than filling them in.

A normal charge starts well below 4 V, climbs through the constant-current stage, then holds near 4.2 V while the current tapers. Five records lack that climb: B0005, B0006 and B0007 at cycle 31 contain implausible initial voltage spikes above 8 V, while B0018 at cycles 46 and 56 resemble top-up charges. Those records contain no samples in the 3.8 to 4.18 V climb at 0.5 A or more (a normal record has hundreds), so the rules for the CC stage and the charge-curve peak have nothing to measure.

What the source pipeline did, and what this analysis does

The source pipeline treats a cycle as invalid when t_cc is under 100 s or dq_dv_peak is under 0.1 Ah/V. It then replaces the charging numbers by interpolation between neighbouring cycles. Because of the order it tests its columns, {{n_filled_cells}} cells change (t_cc, t_cv and dq_dv_peak in five cycles) and the 4 V fallback for the peak voltage stays.

This analysis keeps those five rows and their capacity labels, but leaves all four charging numbers blank ({{n_masked_cells}} cells). A filled value looks measured; a blank one says nothing. Every chart states how many cycles it used.

As a check on the whole processing path, the saved audit rebuilt the source CSV from the raw files: {{recon_columns}} columns, {{recon_mismatches}} mismatches.

Step 5 · Data processing

Does the table follow its own rules, and what is known when?

{{n_rules}} reusable rules pass, the exceptions are written down, and only the four charging features are cleanly known at the end of charge.

A data contract is a written list of rules the table must follow: unique cycle keys, SoH equal to capacity ÷ 2 Ah × 100, flagged values blank, arrays of matching length, times in increasing order, and each cycle linked to the right raw operations. A program checks them and reports exceptions instead of hiding them.

Exceptions that are written down

  • {{n_flagged}} cycles have a charge-quality flag (chapter 4).
  • The source CSV fills {{n_filled_cells}} feature cells that this analysis leaves out.
  • One unused raw charge operation (B0018, operation 114) contains six non-finite sensor samples. It is not selected for any cycle, so no cycle is affected and nothing was repaired.
  • SoH above 100% is accepted: B0006 has {{above_100}} such cycles. The 2 Ah figure is a reference, not a limit, and clipping would change the labels.

When is each number known?

No prediction moment has been chosen for this project, so two are shown: when the charge ends, and when the discharge starts.

  • Charging features are recorded by the end of charge, but only for the {{n_complete}} unflagged cycles.
  • Temperature rise needs the whole discharge. Voltage drop needs the moment the load switches on, roughly 19 to 36 seconds into the discharge.
  • Re and Rct come from the nearest sweep. {{eis_after_charge}} of those start after charging ends, and a sweep that started earlier is not certified either, because the files give no completion time.
  • The interval ends when the discharge starts, so it is final only by then, and it includes charging time.

What the table says

Step 6 · Analysis

How does health change over a cell’s life?

SoH falls from {{soh_max}}% to {{soh_min}}% across the four cells, with jumps after long idle gaps and a few values above 100%.

Before relating anything to health, look at health itself. Because SoH is exactly capacity ÷ 2 × 100, correlations with SoH and with capacity are identical.

Step 7 · Analysis

Which measurements move with health?

Charging duration and the charge-curve peak track health most closely: ρ {{rho_t_cc_min}} to {{rho_t_cc_max}} and {{rho_dq_dv_peak_min}} to {{rho_dq_dv_peak_max}} within each cell. The interval between discharges does not.

Two numbers summarise how a feature relates to SoH. Spearman ρ asks whether they consistently rise together (positive) or one falls as the other rises (negative). Pearson r asks the same for straight-line relationships. Both run from −1 to +1.

What to take from it

  1. t_cc and dq_dv_peak are the clearest candidates for further checking. Per cell, t_cc has ρ {{rho_t_cc_min}} to {{rho_t_cc_max}} and dq_dv_peak {{rho_dq_dv_peak_min}} to {{rho_dq_dv_peak_max}}. That is an association within these cells, not evidence they would transfer.
  2. Those two overlap. Chapter 10 shows how much.
  3. t_cv and the peak voltage rise as health falls: ρ {{rho_t_cv_min}} to {{rho_t_cv_max}} and {{rho_v_at_peak_min}} to {{rho_v_at_peak_max}} per cell. Treat that as an observed direction rather than a sign to assume in advance.
  4. The interval between discharges is inconsistent: ρ {{rho_rest_time_hours_min}} to {{rho_rest_time_hours_max}}. It includes charging time, so this cannot show whether true idle rest matters.
  5. Temperature rise, voltage drop and the resistances are strongly associated within cells (for example temperature rise {{rho_delta_T_max_min}} to {{rho_delta_T_max_max}}), but they come from data recorded after charging ends.

The pooled column is lower for several features because it mixes differences between cells with changes inside one cell.

Step 8 · Analysis

Is it the aging trend doing the work?

Neighbouring cycles resemble each other almost perfectly, so a strong correlation partly reflects that everything drifts with age.

Think of it this way. Consecutive pages of a book share context. Neighbouring cycles are also related; they are not the same as many independent batteries.

Autocorrelation compares a series with itself a few cycles later. Switching from the measurement itself to its cycle-to-cycle changes removes most of the shared trend.

For capacity at lag 1, levels correlate {{lag1_level_min}} to {{lag1_level_max}}, but changes only {{lag1_diff_min}} to {{lag1_diff_max}}. The next chart does the same comparison for every feature against capacity.

For t_cc the correlation of changes falls to {{chg_t_cc_min}} to {{chg_t_cc_max}}, and for dq_dv_peak to {{chg_dq_dv_peak_min}} to {{chg_dq_dv_peak_max}}. After removing what cycle number explains, ranks still agree: {{adj_t_cc_min}} to {{adj_t_cc_max}} for t_cc and {{adj_dq_dv_peak_min}} to {{adj_dq_dv_peak_max}} for dq_dv_peak. So part of the strong association is the shared trend, and a smaller part is a relationship that persists. These are different diagnostics, not proof of a mechanism.

Step 9 · Analysis

Could a curved pattern hide from correlation?

Spearman sees only steadily rising or falling patterns. Mutual information can also see curves. For these data the two point the same way.

Think of it this way. A U-shaped road goes downhill, then uphill. One overall direction cancels out even though the path has a clear shape.

Mutual information (MI) measures how much one quantity narrows down another, curved relationships included. It is in nats, has no sign, and is not a percentage.

Step 10 · Analysis

Do the features repeat each other?

The four charging features overlap heavily, so they are not four independent clues.

Think of it this way. Four witnesses who repeat each other are not four independent clues. A high overlap score warns that a straight-line formula may struggle to separate their individual contributions.

The variance inflation factor (VIF) measures that overlap for the four charging features. Within a cell, t_cc and dq_dv_peak have ρ of {{pair_tcc_dq_min}} to {{pair_tcc_dq_max}} with each other.

The next map shows all nine features, ordered so similar ones sit together.

A high VIF is about redundancy and unstable coefficients. It is not an instruction to delete a feature, and the analysis applies no cut-off.

Step 11 · Analysis

Do cycles fall into natural groups?

Three groups (an illustration, not an estimate of the true number) mostly follow age but mix cells, and one group holds only B0006.

Cycles are grouped on the nine standardized features only. SoH, capacity, cycle number and battery ID never decide the groups; they are used afterwards to describe them. A principal-component map squeezes nine numbers into two axes so the groups can be drawn.

With three groups the sizes are {{cluster_sizes}}. Two of the three mix cells; the third contains only B0006. That combines changes within a cell with differences between cells, so it does not define health classes. The full clustering report offers per-cell refits and up to six groups.

Step 12 · Analysis

Would a new cell look like the ones we have?

Each cell covers a different range, and late-life values often fall outside early-life ranges, so what counts as “seen before” depends on the stage.

Think of it this way. Learning from small water tanks may leave you unprepared for a much larger one. Coverage asks which situations your observations actually include.

A value outside another cell’s range shows a gap in experience. It is not a measured error, and ageing can legitimately change a distribution.

Step 13 · Analysis

How much could a relationship vary, and when do we refuse to say?

Of {{unc_records}} cases, {{unc_conditional}} get an exploratory interval and {{unc_skipped}} are withheld because the assumptions look shaky.

Think of it this way. Shuffling short passages instead of single words keeps some local context. A block bootstrap keeps neighbouring cycles together, while still relying on assumptions.

This step studies cycle-to-cycle changes. It re-draws short runs of neighbouring pairs many times and reports a 95% interval only if the checks pass. A withheld interval is information: it says the assumptions are doubtful for that case.

Part 3

Reading it responsibly

Step 14 · Reading the results

What can we say, and what can’t we?

Within these four cells some features track health closely. The analysis cannot say they would work on a new cell, or at the end of charge.

✓ Supported, descriptively

  • Charging duration and the charge-curve peak rise and fall with health in every cell.
  • The four charging features overlap, so they are not independent clues.
  • Neighbouring cycles are near-duplicates, and part of every strong association is the shared aging trend.
  • Five cycles need special handling, and missing values are explicit.
  • Several inputs depend on information recorded after charging ends.

✗ Not supported

  • That any feature would predict the health of a new cell. All four cells have been examined; none is held out.
  • That temperature rise, voltage drop, Re or Rct can be used at the end of charge.
  • That the interval between discharges has a consistent effect: it changes sign between cells and includes charging time.
  • Any statement about cause, or a universal ranking of features.

Before training anything

  1. Check t_cc and dq_dv_peak against the raw waveforms (the explorer in chapter 3 is a start).
  2. Decide how to handle the five flagged cycles, and fix the alignment of what is known when.
  3. Define actual idle rest instead of the interval between discharges.
  4. Obtain new validation data: a cell never used to choose features.
  5. Keep every result described as exploratory until then.

The project’s app puts these views next to the source repository’s five models, and its Model step opens with notes that carry these limits forward.
